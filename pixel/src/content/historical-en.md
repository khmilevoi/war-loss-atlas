# history:ww1
## First World War

### Period
1914–1918

### Region
Europe and other theatres

### Context
The Western Front extended from Belgium across northern France to the Swiss border. It was one of several theatres of the war.

The US National Archives cites an estimate of about 16 million deaths during the war. Its publication leaves the composition of the total and the method of calculation unspecified.

### Sources
- [US National Archives — death estimate](https://www.archives.gov/press/press-releases/2009/nr09-77)
- [UK National Army Museum — First World War](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/first-world-war#the-western-front)

# history:ww1/beersheba
## Battle of Beersheba

### Period
31 October 1917

### Region
Beersheba, Ottoman Palestine

### Context
The Australian 4th Light Horse Brigade charged Ottoman trenches as part of the Allied attack on Beersheba. Taking the town helped the Allies outflank the Ottoman line that stretched to Gaza.

### Sources
- [Australian War Memorial — Attack on Beersheba](https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/E84287)

# history:ww2
## Second World War

### Period
1939–1945

### Region
Europe, North Africa, Asia and the Pacific

### Context
The war in Europe began with Germany’s invasion of Poland. The Allies fought Germany, Italy and Japan across several continents. In August 1945, the United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

The UK National Army Museum estimates that more than 65 million people died. Its account includes civilian deaths from bombing, famine and genocide. The US Holocaust Memorial Museum publishes a separate estimate of about 55 million. The atlas uses the UK museum’s lower bound for the main estimate.

### Sources
- [UK National Army Museum — Second World War](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/second-world-war)
- [US Holocaust Memorial Museum — World War II](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/world-war-ii-in-depth)

# history:us-civil
## American Civil War

### Period
1861–1865

### Region
United States of America

### Context
The Union fought the Confederacy. In the later stages of the war, their armies fought a series of costly battles in Virginia, including Cold Harbor and Petersburg.

The US National Park Service cites research estimating that at least 750,000 people died as a direct result of the war. This is a lower bound. The estimate covers more than battlefield deaths.

### Sources
- [US National Park Service — Civil War overview](https://www.nps.gov/civilwar/overview.htm)

# history:korea
## Korean War

### Period
1950–1953

### Region
Korean Peninsula

### Context
North Korean troops invaded South Korea in June 1950. UN-led forces fought on the southern side and Chinese forces on the northern side. An armistice was signed in July 1953.

The UK National Army Museum estimates about 2 million deaths among North and South Korean civilians. Its figure covers only civilians from the two Koreas.

### Sources
- [UK National Army Museum — Korean War](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/korean-war)

# history:ww2/hiroshima
## Atomic bombing of Hiroshima

### Period
6 August 1945

### Region
Hiroshima, Japan

### Context
On the morning of 6 August, the United States dropped an atomic bomb on Hiroshima. The explosion above the city destroyed buildings, started fires and exposed people to radiation. The illustrative marker is near Shima Hospital, below the estimated point of detonation.

The City of Hiroshima estimates that about 140,000 people died by the end of December 1945. The precise toll remains unknown. The estimate covers deaths in Hiroshima in the first months after the bombing. It excludes subsequent effects beyond the cutoff.

# history:ww2/nagasaki
## Atomic bombing of Nagasaki

### Period
9 August 1945

### Region
Urakami, Nagasaki, Japan

### Context
At 11:02 local time, an American atomic bomb exploded above the Urakami area. The blast, heat and radiation killed many people. The illustrative marker identifies the Peace Park area where the city places the estimated hypocentre.

The Committee for the Preservation of Atomic Bomb Artifacts estimated that 73,884 people had died by the end of December 1945. The estimate was published in 1950 and covers deaths in Nagasaki within that period.

- [City of Nagasaki — bombing archive](https://nagasakipeace.jp/en/search/record/photo/part_2_01.html)
- [City of Nagasaki — death estimate](https://nagasakipeace.jp/en/reference/materials/kids/higai.html)
